How Maggie edits

Three marks, the same ones an editor would use. Every change she makes to your resume is one of these.

^led the redesign

She adds what the job asks for

Only from your confirmed facts, in the employer’s own wording, with the source marked next to it.

various initiatives

She cuts what doesn’t help

Vague lines go. What stays is reordered so the most relevant work is read first.

work rights?

She circles what needs you

A question only you can answer stops her. It shows up first on your Today screen.

What Maggie will never do

Invent a role or skill you don’t have

Every line traces back to a fact you confirmed.

Type your password

At a sign-in page she stops and hands the form to you.

Guess your work rights or demographics

Those answers come from you or your saved answers, never from her.

Send more than you allow

A daily limit you set, and a pause that works at once.

Why it could winNobody else in the category can own this, and it explains the product without a diagram: you see the edit, the reason and the source in one picture. Freshers get the idea in 5 seconds; professionals see restraint and evidence.
RiskRed pencil reads as “correction” and can feel like being graded. The red is kept for marks on the resume only, never for the person’s own mistakes, and errors in the app keep their own red with an icon and words.
What it needsKalam is used only for the margin notes, never for anything you must read to act. The resume itself must look like a real, good resume, so a designer should check the sample with recruiters.
